The framework

Ten fronts where life actually gets attacked

Not ten steps to a better you. Ten places where people quietly lose ground — and a way to pray about each one that is specific enough to be answerable. Start wherever you already are.

Where are you carrying the most weight?

Thirty statements, one at a time, about two minutes. This is not a test and there is no score to be ashamed of — it simply shows which fronts are loudest in your life right now so you know where to start.

This stays on your device. We never see your answers.

1

The Fire

When prayer has gone cold and you do not want to pray at all.

There is a strange story in 2 Kings 6. A group of men are cutting timber by the Jordan when one of them swings an axe and the iron head flies off the handle into the river. He cries out — not because he lost a tool, but because the tool was borrowed. Elisha asks where it fell, throws a stick into the water, and the iron floats. That is a picture of what happens to prayer. You do not usually decide to stop praying. You swing, and somewhere in the motion the head comes off. You are still going through the movements — church, grace before dinner, a sentence at bedtime — but the weight is gone. Nothing is cutting. Here is the mercy in the story: the man did not manufacture a new axe head. He told the prophet where it fell. That is all. The recovery was God's work, not his. If prayer has gone cold, you do not need to feel guilty enough to fix it. Guilt has never once restarted a prayer life. What restarts it is honesty about the exact place the iron went in. "I stopped after the diagnosis." "I stopped when the answer did not come." "I stopped when I got busy and did not miss it." Tell God where it fell. That sentence is already a prayer, and it is enough to begin with. He is not standing at the riverbank with His arms crossed, waiting for you to dive in and find it yourself.
  • 2 Kings 6:5–6Alas, my master! For it was borrowed. The man of God said, "Where did it fall?" He showed him the place.
  • Revelation 2:4–5But I have this against you, that you left your first love. Remember therefore from where you have fallen, and repent and do the first works.
  • Psalm 51:10Create in me a clean heart, God. Renew a right spirit within me.
  • Psalm 42:1–2As the deer pants for the water brooks, so my soul pants after you, God. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God.

Where exactly did it fall? Name the month, the event, or the disappointment where your praying went quiet.

A prayer you can pray tonight

God, I am not going to pretend. Prayer has gone cold and I have not wanted to come. I am telling You where it fell — and You already know. I am not asking for a feeling. I am asking You to do what I cannot do for myself: make the iron float. Bring the weight back. I will keep showing up in the meantime, even empty. Amen.

2

The Target

You are fighting your spouse, your boss, your kid — the wrong opponent.

Paul writes that our wrestling is not against flesh and blood. Read that slowly, because it is not a poetic flourish. It is a correction of aim. Most of the energy in a hard season goes into the visible person. The husband who will not talk. The manager who took credit. The daughter who slammed the door. You rehearse arguments in the shower. You compose the message you will never send. And at the end of all that expended force, nothing has moved — because force was applied to the wrong thing. This is the front where you learn to stop swinging at people. That does not mean the person did nothing. It does not mean you excuse harm, tolerate abuse, or pretend a real problem is imaginary. Naming a spiritual dimension has never been a reason to stay in a dangerous house or to skip the hard conversation. It means you refuse to treat a human being as your enemy, and you start asking a different question: what is actually going on underneath this, and what would it look like to bring that to God instead of to the argument? There is a practical test. Before you speak to the person, speak to God about them — by name, out loud, for two minutes. Notice what changes in you before anything changes in them. Very often the sentence you were going to lead with is not the sentence you end up saying. We are not fighting people. We are asking a victorious Christ to work in a situation bigger than the two of us.
  • Ephesians 6:12For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against the principalities, against the powers, against the world’s rulers of the darkness of this age.
  • 2 Corinthians 10:3–4For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh; for the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh.
  • Ephesians 6:18With all prayer and requests, praying at all times in the Spirit, and being watchful to this end in all perseverance.
  • Colossians 2:15Having stripped the principalities and the powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

Whose face comes to mind when you think about your current fight? What would it change to pray for them by name before you speak to them?

A prayer you can pray tonight

Lord, I have been swinging at a person. I name them now — not as my enemy, but as someone You love and someone I do not know how to reach. Take the fight out of my hands. Show me what is really happening here, and give me the restraint to bring it to You before I bring it to them. You have already won this. Help me act like it. Amen.

3

The Name

Who God says you are, when shame says otherwise.

Read the first chapter of Ephesians with a pen. Underline every phrase that describes what is already true of a person in Christ. Chosen. Adopted. Redeemed. Forgiven. Sealed. Every one of them is past tense or settled fact. Not one is a reward for a good week. Then notice what Paul prays for after listing them. He does not pray that they would receive these things. He prays that the eyes of their heart would be enlightened so that they would know what they already have. That gap — between what is true of you and what you feel is true of you — is where most spiritual discouragement lives. Shame is not the same as conviction. Conviction is specific: it names a thing you did, it points toward repair, and it always leaves a door open. Shame is global: it names what you are, it points nowhere, and it locks the door from the outside. If the voice in your head says "you always" or "you never" or "you are the kind of person who," you are not being convicted. You are being accused. The practical work on this front is small and repeated. Write one sentence Scripture says about you on a card. Put it where you get dressed. Say it out loud when the accusation shows up. Not as a slogan — as testimony, said out loud in your own voice, in a room where only God hears you. You are not talking yourself into something. You are agreeing with what is already recorded.
  • Ephesians 1:4–5Even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world... having predestined us for adoption as children through Jesus Christ to himself.
  • Ephesians 1:18Having the eyes of your hearts enlightened, that you may know what is the hope of his calling.
  • 1 John 3:1Behold, how great a love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of God!
  • Romans 8:15–16You received the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, "Abba! Father!" The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit that we are children of God.

What sentence about yourself do you most believe on a bad day? What does Scripture put in its place?

A prayer you can pray tonight

Father, I have believed a sentence about myself that You never said. I bring it into the light instead of carrying it quietly. Open the eyes of my heart to what is already true — that I am chosen, adopted, and Yours, and that this did not depend on my performance today. Let me hear Your voice more clearly than the accusation. Amen.

4

The House

Marriage, children, parents — the people under your roof.

Joshua stands in front of a nation and says: as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD. It is one of the most quoted verses in Christian homes, usually printed on a wall. It is worth noticing that it is a decision, not a description. He is not reporting on how his family is doing. He is planting a flag. Praying for your household is the most common reason people begin praying seriously, and the most common place they get discouraged, because the people you love the most are the people whose change you can measure the least honestly. A few things help. Pray by name, not by category. "Bless my family" is a wish. "God, my son Marcos, at nineteen, angry and not talking to me — meet him" is a prayer. Pray for one thing at a time and stay there. A month of praying one specific thing over one person does more than a year of praying everything over everyone. Pray in the room. There is something about standing in the doorway of a sleeping child, or in the kitchen after everyone has gone to bed, that a prayer list on a phone cannot reproduce. And pray for yourself in the same breath. It is very hard to pray daily for someone and stay bitter toward them. That is not a technique. It is just what happens. Two warnings. Praying for your marriage is not a substitute for the honest conversation, and it is never a reason to stay somewhere unsafe. If there is violence, fear, or coercive control in your home, please read our safety page first.
  • Joshua 24:15As for me and my house, we will serve Yahweh.
  • Psalm 127:1Unless Yahweh builds the house, they who build it labor in vain.
  • Deuteronomy 6:6–7These words shall be on your heart; and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house.
  • 3 John 1:4I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth.

Name one person under your roof and one specific thing. Not everything about them — one thing, for thirty days.

A prayer you can pray tonight

God, I bring You my house — not as a category, but by name. You know the exact place each of them is standing tonight. I am asking You to meet them there in a way I cannot arrange. Where I have contributed to the distance, show me plainly and give me the humility to go first. And build this house, because I have been trying to build it in my own strength and I am tired. Amen.

5

The Ledger

Guilt, regret, and the past you keep re-reading.

Some people carry a ledger. Not a real one — a mental record of every failure, kept open, re-read at three in the morning. The entries are usually accurate. That is what makes it so convincing. Scripture does something strange with that ledger. It does not say the entries were false. It says the account was settled by someone else, and then it uses spatial language that is almost aggressive: as far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us. Not filed. Not suspended pending good behavior. Removed, to a distance that cannot be measured, because east and west never meet. Paul, who had a genuinely terrible page in his own ledger, describes his practice in one line: forgetting the things which are behind, and stretching forward to the things which are before. Note that he calls it something he does, not something he feels. Nobody wakes up having forgotten. You choose, repeatedly, not to re-read. Practically, this front is about what you do with the page when it opens. Confess what is actually yours — specifically, once, out loud. Make repair where repair is possible and wise. Then, when the memory returns, do not argue with it and do not relitigate it. Say out loud what is now true: this is forgiven, it was paid for, and I am not going back to court over a closed case. If you find you cannot let it go, that is worth saying to God plainly, and often worth saying to a wise person too. Some ledgers need a counsellor's help to close, and that is not a failure of faith.
  • Psalm 103:12As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us.
  • Romans 8:1There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.
  • Philippians 3:13–14Forgetting the things which are behind, and stretching forward to the things which are before, I press on toward the goal.
  • 1 John 1:9If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

What page opens at three in the morning? Has it actually been confessed — or only replayed?

A prayer you can pray tonight

Lord, I keep re-reading a page You have already closed. I confess what is truly mine, plainly and once. Where I can repair something, make me willing. And where the memory returns after that, teach me not to argue with it — because You did not suspend the case, You settled it. Let me sleep like someone who has been forgiven. Amen.

6

The Night

Fear, anxiety, the three-in-the-morning spiral.

Philippians 4:6 is quoted at anxious people constantly, usually as a rebuke. "Be anxious for nothing" gets used as if it were a command to feel differently, which is a command nobody can obey. Read the rest of the sentence. It is not "be anxious for nothing, because worry is a sin." It is "be anxious for nothing, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God." It is not an instruction about feelings. It is an instruction about direction. Anxiety is energy pointed inward and in circles; the verse redirects it outward, toward Someone, with words. And notice what is promised. Not that the situation resolves. Not that the fear evaporates. That the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus. Guard is a military word. A sentry does not remove the enemy. He stands at the gate of your mind while the night goes on. Practically, at 3am: sit up, turn on one small light, and say it out loud. Silent prayer at 3am tends to lose to the spiral, because the spiral is also silent and it is faster than you are. Speaking gives your mind one thing to follow. Then name the fear specifically — "I am afraid the scan comes back bad" — instead of the fog of general dread. Then hand it over in one sentence. Then say one true thing about God that is not about your situation at all. One more thing, and it matters. Prayer and a doctor are not rivals. If anxiety is running your days, please talk to a professional. Doing so is not small faith; it is stewardship.
  • Philippians 4:6–7In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.
  • Psalm 4:8In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep, for you alone, Yahweh, make me live in safety.
  • Isaiah 41:10Don't be afraid, for I am with you. Don't be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you.
  • 1 Peter 5:7Casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

What is the actual sentence underneath the dread? Say it plainly — the vague version is always heavier than the named one.

A prayer you can pray tonight

God, it is late and my mind will not stop. Here is the fear, named out loud instead of circling: [say it]. I am handing it to You, and I will probably have to hand it over again in ten minutes, and that is all right. I am not asking to stop feeling it tonight. I am asking You to stand at the gate of my mind while I sleep. You are awake anyway. Amen.

7

The Eyes

Purity, screens, and what you feed yourself in private.

Job says he made a covenant with his eyes. The Psalmist says he will set no worthless thing before his eyes. Both are talking about a decision made in advance, before the moment of temptation, when the decision is still cheap. That is the whole practical insight of this front. Almost nobody wins this fight at eleven at night with a phone in their hand. The fight is won at eight in the evening, when you decide where the phone sleeps. Two things need saying honestly. First, this is not only a men's issue and it is not only about pornography. It is about what you feed yourself when nobody is watching — the endless scroll, the comparison feed, the outrage cycle, the show you watch to stop feeling. What goes in shapes what comes out, and most of us are eating badly in private. Second, shame does not work here. It has been tried for a very long time and its record is perfect: it has never once produced lasting change. Shame drives the behaviour deeper and adds secrecy, which is the actual engine of the problem. What does work is the opposite of secrecy — confession to one trustworthy person, and structural change made when you are calm. So pray, yes. And then put the phone in the kitchen, and tell one safe person the truth, and ask a professional for help if it has a grip you cannot break. Grace is not the alternative to practical steps; grace is what makes practical steps survivable.
  • Job 31:1I made a covenant with my eyes; how then should I look lustfully at a young woman?
  • Psalm 101:3I will set no vile thing before my eyes.
  • Matthew 5:8Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.
  • Romans 13:14But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, for its lusts.

What decision could you make at 8pm that would make the 11pm decision unnecessary?

A prayer you can pray tonight

God, You see what I feed myself when no one is watching, and I am not going to describe it as less than it is. I do not want to fight this with shame again — that has never worked. Give me one decision I can make early, and the honesty to tell one safe person the truth. Make my private life match my public one. Amen.

8

The Weight

Money, work, debt, a job lost, a body tired.

Jesus talks about money and worry in the same breath, which is not an accident. Look at the birds, He says. Consider the lilies. Then: which of you by being anxious can add one moment to his lifespan? That question is doing something specific. It is not minimizing the bill. It is pointing out that worry, whatever else it is, is not productive. It feels like work. It generates the physical sensations of work — the tight chest, the sleeplessness, the running numbers. But at the end of a night of it, nothing has been built. Let us be clear about what this front is not. It is not a promise that God will make you wealthy. Any teaching that measures your faith by your bank balance has quietly made God an employee and money the boss. Some of the most faithful people in Scripture were poor, and stayed poor, and were not being punished. What Scripture actually promises is presence and provision — "my God will supply every need of yours" — which is not the same as every want, and which has been true in a hundred lean seasons that did not look like rescue at the time. So on this front you pray two things at once. You ask plainly and specifically for what you need, because Jesus told you to ask for daily bread and daily is a very short horizon. And you ask for your grip to loosen — that the money would stop being the thing that decides whether you are all right. Then do the practical work in the daylight: the budget, the phone call, the application, the honest conversation with the person you owe. Prayer is not a substitute for the phone call. It is what makes you able to make it.
  • Matthew 6:27, 33Which of you by being anxious, can add one moment to his lifespan? But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well.
  • Philippians 4:19My God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.
  • Matthew 6:11Give us today our daily bread.
  • Hebrews 13:5Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have, for he has said, "I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you."

What is the number that is actually keeping you awake? Say it out loud to God, exactly, instead of carrying it as a fog.

A prayer you can pray tonight

Father, here is the number, said out loud: [say it]. I am asking plainly, because You told me to ask for bread one day at a time. I am also asking for something harder — loosen my grip. Do not let this amount decide whether I am all right. Give me the courage for the practical steps I have been avoiding, and give me enough for today. Amen.

9

The Wound

What someone did to you, and forgiveness you cannot manufacture.

From the cross, Jesus says: Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing. It is worth noticing what He does not do. He does not say it did not happen. He does not say it did not matter. He does not tell the people who hurt Him that they are fine. He hands them to His Father. That is the shape of biblical forgiveness, and it is different from what people usually mean by the word. Forgiveness is releasing your right to collect the debt. It is not saying the debt was imaginary. It is not pretending you were not hurt. It is not automatic reconciliation — reconciliation takes two people and requires changed behaviour, and there are relationships where trust cannot yet be safely rebuilt. You can forgive someone completely and still, wisely, keep a door closed. It is also rarely a single moment. Most people forgive the same wound many times, because the memory keeps presenting the bill again. Handing it over again is not failure. It is the practice. And it usually cannot be manufactured by effort. If you have tried to force yourself to feel forgiving and failed, you are not unusually hard-hearted. Start further back: tell God honestly what was done and how much it cost you. Ask Him to do in you what you cannot do in yourself. Then, when the memory returns, hand the person over rather than replaying the case. If the wound is deep — abuse, betrayal, violence — please involve a wise counsellor. Some wounds need a witness, and healing that takes professional help is still healing.
  • Luke 23:34Jesus said, "Father, forgive them, for they don’t know what they are doing."
  • Ephesians 4:32And be kind to one another, tender hearted, forgiving each other, just as God also in Christ forgave you.
  • Colossians 3:13Bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any; even as Christ forgave you, so you also do.
  • Romans 12:19Don't seek revenge yourselves, beloved, but give place to God's wrath.

What exactly did it cost you? Name the cost before you try to release it — you cannot forgive a debt you have never counted.

A prayer you can pray tonight

Lord, You know what was done and You know what it cost me, and I am not going to shrink it to make this prayer easier. I cannot manufacture forgiveness, so I am asking You to do in me what I cannot do myself. I hand this person to You instead of holding the debt. If I have to hand them over again tomorrow, I will. Keep me from becoming what hurt me. Amen.

10

The Table

Division between believers — friendly fire in the church and the family.

On the night before He died, with limited time and everything to say, Jesus prayed that His followers would be one. Of all the things He could have asked for them, He asked for that. Then look at what actually happens. Churches split over things nobody outside the room would understand. Families stop speaking over an inheritance or a comment at a wedding. Ministries with the same mission treat each other as competitors. And the damage is done almost entirely by people who love God. Scripture describes a peace that moves in three directions: peace with God, which is settled by Christ; the peace of God, which guards you in the middle of trouble; and peace with others, which is the one that requires something of you. That third one has a realistic clause attached. If it is possible, as much as it depends on you, be at peace with all men. If it is possible — because it is not always. As much as it depends on you — because the other half does not. The prayer work here is uncomfortable and specific: pray for the person you are divided from, by name, and ask God to bless them, not to prove you right. It is nearly impossible to pray a genuine blessing over someone and keep nursing the case against them. That is exactly why it works, and exactly why we avoid it. Then, where it depends on you, make the call. The one you have been composing in your head for a year.
  • John 17:21That they may all be one; even as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be one in us.
  • Psalm 133:1See how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to live together in unity!
  • Romans 12:18If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.
  • Ephesians 4:3Being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

Who are you divided from that you could bless out loud tonight — without needing to be proved right first?

A prayer you can pray tonight

Jesus, on Your last night You prayed we would be one, and I have not been helping. I name the person I am divided from and I ask You to bless them — genuinely, not as a strategy. Take my need to be proved right. Show me the part that depends on me, and give me the humility to move first without waiting to be met halfway. Amen.
